## Product direction

**Confirmed direction: detect possible environmental drift through a decision-specific assumption framework and changes in decision terms.** Private credit is the first demonstration domain; the broader workflow could apply wherever an original decision, its assumptions and governing terms can be reassessed as new evidence arrives. Transfer to other domains remains a hypothesis to test.

The central question is: **“Given what we know now, would the original decision and its terms still fit the environment?”** Detection is the purpose. A generated package is a comparison instrument, and an adopted amendment is an observed response that can provide evidence of drift. Neither is presumed to be the correct remedy or a training target for an optimal lending decision.

The intended private-credit workflow is:

1. Load a company's borrower profile, underwriting memo, financial model and actual covenant package.
2. Have Nemotron generate a company-specific assumption register and relationship map, preserving approved claims and labeling inferred claims separately.
3. Save the framework, company reports, operative package versions and explicit lender judgment guidelines. Keep deal-specific beliefs, lender policy and historical lender responses distinct.
4. Break new reports into claims and stated implications, consult the borrower library early, and test possible unstated implications through targeted research. Aggregate distinct economic effects, attribute them to assumptions and covenant duties, then have Nemotron construct a complete fresh candidate package using the updated information under fixed guidelines.
5. Compare substantive terms with the original and current approved packages. Trace meaningful differences to evidence and affected assumptions to detect possible drift; equivalent wording is not a drift signal.
6. Use dated historical evidence and actual lender package/covenant changes to learn which response patterns may indicate which environmental changes. An adopted amendment is an observed detection signal, not proof of drift or a correct answer the model must reproduce.
7. Present the drift assessment, package differences, evidence and unresolved attribution for analyst review. Preserve the full history. Adopting a package remains a separate explicit decision and is not required for detection.

The proposed distinction is a reusable framework native to each decision, connecting original assumptions, new evidence and observed or reconstructed changes in terms. Lender guidelines constrain model judgment; historical responses may improve detection. Better accuracy, earlier warning, analyst time savings, cross-domain transfer and a commercial advantage over Lumonic have **not** been established. Full package generation/comparison, guideline controls and learning from historical responses remain proposed capabilities; today's implementation boundary is below.

### Report libraries and the presentation sequence

Each company needs a separately scoped report history linked to its own profile, assumptions, operative terms and past decisions. Reports can include financial updates, management commentary, incident reviews, customer developments and industry analyses. A shared industry report can be associated with multiple companies, with an independent attribution for each; it must not mix borrower-specific facts or imply the same outcome for every company.

The intended presentation flow is:

1. Show the borrower's current approved package and assumption assessments before the new report.
2. Introduce a dated report that was unavailable to the model at the previous review point.
3. Have Nemotron cite relevant passages, identify exposed assumptions and relationships, and assess implications for the operative covenants.
4. Save the report and resulting analysis in that company's history, updating its current assessment without erasing the baseline.
5. Explain what changed, what remains unknown and the suggested response: no action, monitoring, a request for more evidence or review of a proposed amendment.
6. If the analyst approves an amendment, record a new package version and effective date. Otherwise keep the current package active and retain the analysis and decision history.

The timeline should distinguish three connected layers: **reports and evidence**, **credit assessments**, and **approved contractual packages**. New reports can change the assessment without causing an amendment. Reports that support the existing view or have no material effect should remain visible in the history without creating false divergence markers.

An industry trend is not automatically a borrower fact or a covenant breach. For example, an industry churn report may expose renewal assumptions, but borrower-specific customer evidence and the applicable contract conditions are needed to establish an actual covenant failure. Nemotron should identify those evidence gaps explicitly.

### Article reasoning and proposed review-priority rubric

**Direction recorded September 19, 2026; not implemented or validated.** Nemotron should combine article argument mapping, the borrower's exposure and assumption relationships, and targeted hypothesis testing. The library supplies context throughout the analysis, not only at the final comparison.

1. **Decompose the report.** Extract its main point, underlying data, supporting points and explicitly stated implications. Preserve source passages, attribution, dates, qualifications, negation and whether a statement is an observation, forecast or opinion.
2. **Retrieve borrower context early.** Identify relevant business exposures, approved assumptions, operative covenant definitions, known mitigants and missing company facts before researching indirect effects.
3. **Test unstated implications.** Treat them as hypotheses. Seek supporting and contradicting evidence through actual retrieval from the report library and explicitly connected, eligible sources; model recollection is not research. Consider adverse, beneficial, mitigating and no-material-effect explanations. Even a short report can require research. Stop when a hypothesis is supported, refuted, immaterial or unresolved within a defined search budget, retaining unresolved gaps.
4. **Explain the transmission path.** Trace external development → economic channel → borrower exposure → assumption → covenant implication. Label each connection as source evidence, a company fact, an approved relationship or an explicit model hypothesis. A plausible macroeconomic story alone cannot establish a borrower effect.
5. **Aggregate distinct economic effects.** Group overlapping claims by economic driver and deduplicate related sources. Higher costs, lower margins and lower EBITDA along one causal path are not three independent risk increments. Offset effects only when evidence supports their realization against the same metric, period and scenario; unrelated upside cannot cancel a critical obligation.
6. **Return separate findings.** Preserve assumption exposure, current covenant compliance, future pressure and possible package drift as distinct conclusions. Save the argument map, research evidence, hypotheses, paths, aggregation rationale and gaps with the assessment. Do not invent financial inputs or change operative terms automatically.

The proposed scoring aid is an **ordinal potential-consequence tier**, with separate evidence and applicability fields:

| Dimension | Proposed representation |
|---|---|
| Potential consequence | **0:** supported immaterial effect; **1:** limited implication to monitor; **2:** potentially material; **3:** potentially decision-critical. **Unknown/unassessed** is separate from zero. |
| Evidence support | Direct evidence, supported inference, unresolved hypothesis or conflicting evidence, with reasons. |
| Borrower exposure | Documented, conditional, unknown or demonstrably limited. |
| Direction | Adverse, beneficial, neutral, mixed or unclear. |
| Timing | Current, near-term, longer-term or unknown, retaining applicable dates and deadlines. |
| Covenant finding | A separately reasoned assessment against operative terms and available evidence; never inferred from the consequence tier. |

These dimensions do not form a summed borrower credit score or breach threshold. Do not multiply severity by model confidence: potentially critical but weakly supported effects may require urgent verification. Lender-specific tier criteria, routing rules and evaluation remain to be defined. Version the rubric and lender guidelines; retain the model's rationale and analyst overrides. Evaluate duplicate reports, unrelated positive evidence, unknown exposure and unsupported causal leaps, separately from quotation accuracy. The [master plan](RealityCheck.md#article-argument-mapping-and-research-into-unstated-implications) records the full reasoning loop and its relationship to retrieval; this design does not require a graph database or replacing SQLite.

## Proposed drift detection through package comparison and lender responses

**Status:** The September 19 design now has a scoped candidate-generation/comparison implementation and recorded development results; see the progress overview and [benchmark execution record](RealityCheck-Benchmark-Testing-Plan.md#13-execution-record). Coverage of the complete Crocs scenario and historical-response learning remain further work. FluxRail's adopted amendment instruments are authored fixtures, distinct from generated candidate records. Current results do not establish improved detection or analyst benefit.

The proposed question is: **Knowing what we know today, would we write materially different protections for this borrower?** Nemotron would use the deal-specific framework and new information to construct a complete fresh candidate package under explicit lender guidelines. Comparing it with the original and current packages supplies a generated signal of possible drift. Actual lender amendments supply an observed response signal. The objective is to detect environmental change, not to learn the supposedly correct amendment or optimize lending terms.

Proposed workflow:

1. **Establish the baseline and guidelines.** Preserve the original profile, approved assumptions, original/current packages and lender policy. Specify risk tolerance, materiality, permitted judgment and insufficient-evidence behavior. Version the model, prompts, guidelines and available precedent set used for each comparison.
2. **Generate a complete fresh candidate package.** Reassess the deal using the original context and newly available evidence under those fixed guidelines. A complete candidate does not require changing every clause: retain equivalent protections where justified. Require every substantive change to cite evidence, identify the affected assumption and explain its basis. An unchanged package is a valid result; missing evidence must not become invented facts or unsupported thresholds.
3. **Compare substantive terms.** Compare clause scope, thresholds, definitions, reporting frequency, triggers, permissions and cure periods in a structured representation. Equivalent wording contributes nothing to drift. Compare with origination to show cumulative change and with the current approved package to identify gaps that existing amendments have not addressed.
4. **Assess significance.** Evaluate whether the evidence-driven differences materially change risk coverage, monitoring needs or permitted behavior against predefined materiality criteria. Return material drift, no material drift or insufficient evidence, with reasons and source citations. These criteria remain to be designed and validated. The proposed consequence rubric above supports review priority; its tiers do not establish material drift or a covenant breach.
5. **Interpret observed lender responses.** Relate actual package/covenant changes to the preceding evidence, deal framework and applicable lender policy. Use comparable historical sequences to inform detection, preserving uncertainty about why the action occurred. Keep observed amendments separate from model-generated proposals.
6. **Present the result for review.** Show the candidate package, substantive differences, affected assumptions, evidence, relevant historical responses and drift judgment. Keep generation, detection, review and adoption separate; detection does not require adoption, and a generated proposal does not change operative terms.

**Baseline control:** Generate a comparison proposal using only the original information under the same model, prompt, guidelines and permitted precedent set as the updated-information run. Differences already present in that control can reflect pre-existing gaps or model preferences. Compare the two generated candidates as well as original/current approved packages to isolate sensitivity to new evidence. Freeze these settings within a comparison; record later model/policy/precedent changes separately from borrower drift. Evaluate repeated runs and routine/no-change updates; the control alone does not establish reliability.

For example, newly increased customer concentration could justify moving from quarterly customer reporting to monthly concentration reporting and adding a customer-loss notification trigger. That is a potential material response to changed business exposure. Rewording an equivalent reporting clause is not substantive drift, and a change recommended using origination evidence alone is not newly emerging drift.

This would supplement the existing evidence-to-assumption analysis. Economic deterioration can occur without requiring a covenant revision, and a proposed looser covenant cannot erase a breach under the operative agreement. Package differences are a signal requiring evidence and materiality assessment, not proof of drift by themselves.

### Learning from amendments for detection

Preserve sequences of **dated evidence → affected assumptions → observed lender changes**, including before/after terms, lender rationale where available, applicable policy and alternative explanations. For example, customer losses followed by tighter concentration reporting may teach the system a response pattern associated with weakening revenue stability; it does not establish that the tighter reporting was the right remedy.

An amendment establishes a change in terms. Attributing it to the environment requires context: policy changes, negotiation or administrative revisions can also alter terms, while genuine environmental drift may occur without an amendment. Missing rationale should remain unknown. Learning should improve identification and explanation of possible drift, rather than reward imitation of lender actions.

**Working implementation recommendation:** begin with reviewed historical cases supplied as precedents; decide whether fine-tuning adds value only after evaluation. This is not a confirmed training method or an implemented learning loop. Record when each precedent became available. An amendment can support detection once observed, but must not leak into an earlier-warning test; exclude the held-out case's later actions from its precedent inputs.

Evaluate detection on unseen, dated trajectories with environmental-change judgments assessed separately from amendment adoption. Include amendments without environmental change, drift without amendments, beneficial changes, policy shifts and stable cases. Measure false alerts, missed drift and attribution quality; exact agreement with an adopted amendment is not the success metric.
