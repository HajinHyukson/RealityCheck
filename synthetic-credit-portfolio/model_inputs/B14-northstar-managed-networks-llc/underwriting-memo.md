# Northstar Managed Networks, LLC Underwriting memorandum

Memo B14-UW-2026-01. Synthetic approval and availability: 2026-12-31. Proposed for a private-credit analyst. Dollar amounts are millions. Future scenarios are forecasts known at origination, not later observations.

## Borrower and credit decision

Northstar monitors and administers networks, endpoints, cloud infrastructure, and service desks for mid-sized U.S. enterprises under recurring managed-service agreements. Contract value is durable, but a privileged-access compromise or prolonged monitoring outage could affect many customers simultaneously and accelerate churn.

Northstar opens at 3.51x net leverage on $31.9 million of Covenant EBITDA. The 4.60x maximum allows approximately a 24% EBITDA decline at unchanged net debt, balancing recurring revenue against correlated operational risk. A $6.2 million annual capital-investment cap sits above the $5.0 million base need while limiting cash leakage into speculative platform projects; capitalized development is included in the measure. The $9 million transaction floor preserves incident-response and vendor-payment capacity. Strong recurring revenue and low receivable days aid debt service, but leverage remains material. The principal underwriting vulnerability is a security or monitoring failure that affects multiple customers at once, produces service credits, and weakens renewals before leverage data reveals the full economic impact.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved December 31, 2026 memo's Service Reliability paragraph summarizes synthetic incident history, availability records, and service credits.

### A2

**Claim:** Privileged access is limited, attributable, and revoked promptly enough to contain security risk.

**Baseline evidence:** The memo's Security Controls paragraph describes the identity system, monthly access review, and emergency credential process.

**Review criterion:** Unauthorized access, shared credentials outside the vault, or delayed revocation.

**Contract relationship:** direct; S1, S3, C07. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A3

**Claim:** Core monitoring technology and customer credentials remain controlled by the loan-party group.

**Baseline evidence:** The memo's Technology Ownership paragraph identifies the platform assets, signing keys, and approved subcontractor boundaries.

**Review criterion:** A proposed platform transfer, credential grant, or material subcontracting change.

**Contract relationship:** direct; C06, S3. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A4

**Claim:** Recurring contract cash flow supports debt service while capital investment remains within plan.

**Baseline evidence:** The memo's Cash Flow paragraph links recurring billings, planned capitalized development, and debt service.

**Review criterion:** Service credits rise, recurring contracts shrink, or annual capital investment approaches the cap.

**Contract relationship:** direct; C01, C09, S2. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

## Financial model conventions

The authored balance sheet records trade receivables of $12.285m at 38 collection days, other operating current assets of $15.050m, net fixed/software assets of $34.400m, and historical acquisition goodwill of $142.600m. These are book balances, not collateral recovery estimates. See the model's explicit prepaid/unbilled and deferred-billing simplifications.

- 2025 is comparative income and balance-sheet data; a 2025 cash-flow statement is not fabricated.
- 2026 historical restructuring expense is spread evenly across the four quarters; it is not forecast to recur in 2027.
- Predecessor debt has the same principal and fixed interest rate, with no 2026 amortization; refinancing at par adds no cash or fees.
- 2026 shareholder distributions or contributions reconcile the historical cash target and precede the new agreement.
- Payables and operating accruals (including accrued expenses and deferred customer billings) are held constant; receivables and other operating current assets absorb the stated working-capital change. Deferred customer billings are not forecast separately.
- Other operating current assets comprise prepaid expenses and unbilled contract assets; model movements are explicit, not new EBITDA addbacks.
- Historical goodwill represents earlier acquisitions and stays constant; fixed/software assets roll by capex less depreciation/amortization.
- Restricted cash is borrower-owned cash reserved by contract for operating performance obligations, not client money; it has no matching client-funds liability and no competing lien. It is excluded from leverage netting and available cash.
- Forecast negative cash is an unfunded deficit, not an actual bank balance or assumed new loan; such a scenario is infeasible without corrective financing or spending changes.
- Cash tax is 25% of positive pretax income, with no loss benefit or deferred-tax complexity.
- Q1 realizations are synthetic, with no assumed submission timestamp; every actual delivery obligation must be tested separately.
- Annual interest uses ACT/365F; principal installments are effective December 31, so within-year principal is constant.
