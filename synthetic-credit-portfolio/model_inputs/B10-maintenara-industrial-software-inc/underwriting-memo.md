# Maintenara Industrial Software, Inc. Underwriting memorandum

Memo B10-UW-2026-01. Synthetic approval and availability: 2026-12-31. Proposed for a private-credit analyst. Dollar amounts are millions. Future scenarios are forecasts known at origination, not later observations.

## Borrower and credit decision

Maintenara provides computerized maintenance management, sensor-alert, and spare-parts planning software to process manufacturers under site subscriptions and implementation contracts. Credit quality depends on plant integration uptime, disciplined alert handling, and implementation capacity across geographically dispersed customer sites.

The 4.40x maintenance ceiling permits about a 23% EBITDA decline from 3.40x opening net leverage. A $8.2 million annual capital-investment cap remains above the base plan while limiting a discretionary sensor-platform build that could weaken cash available for interest and three-percent amortization. The $10 million transaction floor reflects slower industrial collections and payroll required for plant deployments. The critical vulnerability is operational credibility: missed critical-equipment alerts or uncontrolled integration changes can impair renewals before leverage reacts. Specialized covenants establish alert-response duties, plant-connector release controls, and implementation-backlog reporting. Acquisition capacity is sized below a year's EBITDA contribution assumptions and requires pro forma leverage below the maintenance limit, reducing integration and liquidity risk.

### A1

**Claim:** Reliable critical-alert delivery and tested connector releases sustain the platform's value to industrial customers.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved underwriting memo's Operational Reliability paragraph reviews synthetic alert logs, connector tests, pilot practice, and customer diligence.

**Review criterion:** A qualifying alert interruption occurs or an expanded connector release bypasses required testing.

**Contract relationship:** direct; S1, S2, C08. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A2

**Claim:** Experienced deployment teams can convert contracted plant backlog into accepted subscription sites without uncontrolled delay.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved underwriting memo's Deployment Capacity paragraph maps contracted sites, staffing, dependencies, acceptance history, and open issues.

**Review criterion:** At-risk site volume materially grows, critical dependencies remain unresolved, or alert service is impaired.

**Contract relationship:** reporting; S1, S3. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A3

**Claim:** Site subscriptions and 64-day collections fund implementation working capital and planned platform investment.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved underwriting memo's Cash Conversion paragraph reconciles milestone invoices, receivable aging, payroll, capital investment, and operating cash needs.

**Review criterion:** Collections slow, backlog conversion weakens, or capital investment approaches its annual cap.

**Contract relationship:** direct; C02, C09, S3. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A4

**Claim:** Opening leverage and cash-preserving transaction limits leave adequate headroom for normal industrial implementation volatility.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved underwriting memo's Capital Structure paragraph presents leverage capacity, amortization, cash floor, and transaction permissions.

**Review criterion:** Leverage rises, cash is diverted, or implementation demands exceed the funded operating plan.

**Contract relationship:** direct; C01, C04, C05, C09. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

## Financial model conventions

The authored balance sheet records trade receivables of $27.529m at 64 collection days, other operating current assets of $20.001m, net fixed/software assets of $47.400m, and historical acquisition goodwill of $166.750m. These are book balances, not collateral recovery estimates. See the model's explicit prepaid/unbilled and deferred-billing simplifications.

- 2025 is comparative income and balance-sheet data; a 2025 cash-flow statement is not fabricated.
- 2026 historical restructuring expense is spread evenly across the four quarters; it is not forecast to recur in 2027.
- Predecessor debt has the same principal and fixed interest rate, with no 2026 amortization; refinancing at par adds no cash or fees.
- 2026 shareholder distributions or contributions reconcile the historical cash target and precede the new agreement.
- Payables and operating accruals (including accrued expenses and deferred customer billings) are held constant; receivables and other operating current assets absorb the stated working-capital change. Deferred customer billings are not forecast separately.
- Other operating current assets comprise prepaid expenses and unbilled contract assets; model movements are explicit, not new EBITDA addbacks.
- Historical goodwill represents earlier acquisitions and stays constant; fixed/software assets roll by capex less depreciation/amortization.
- Restricted cash is borrower-owned cash reserved by contract for operating performance obligations, not client money; it has no matching client-funds liability and no competing lien. It is excluded from leverage netting and available cash.
- Forecast negative cash is an unfunded deficit, not an actual bank balance or assumed new loan; such a scenario is infeasible without corrective financing or spending changes.
- Cash tax is 25% of positive pretax income, with no loss benefit or deferred-tax complexity.
- Q1 realizations are synthetic, with no assumed submission timestamp; every actual delivery obligation must be tested separately.
- Annual interest uses ACT/365F; principal installments are effective December 31, so within-year principal is constant.
