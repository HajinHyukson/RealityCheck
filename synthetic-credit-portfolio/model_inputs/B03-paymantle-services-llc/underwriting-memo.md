# PayMantle Services, LLC Underwriting memorandum

Memo B03-UW-2026-01. Synthetic approval and availability: 2026-12-31. Proposed for a private-credit analyst. Dollar amounts are millions. Future scenarios are forecasts known at origination, not later observations.

## Borrower and credit decision

PayMantle provides payroll processing, employment-tax filing, and workforce payment software to multi-state service businesses under recurring per-employee contracts. Its principal operating risk is timely segregation and transmission of client payroll and tax funds across changing jurisdictional calendars.

The 3.95x leverage ceiling gives about 22% EBITDA decline capacity from 3.06x opening net leverage. A 1.25x annual cash-coverage test adds protection because statutory remittances, platform investment, and scheduled principal must be funded from cash earnings even when reported EBITDA remains positive. Short contractual fee collections support a lower operating-cash requirement, while all client payroll and tax balances remain restricted and excluded from leverage netting. Two-percent amortization limits annual debt service but leaves a refinance obligation at maturity. The dominant vulnerability is execution: one missed tax or payroll transmission can damage client retention and create reimbursement costs. The specialized covenants therefore govern client-fund segregation, remittance exceptions, and jurisdiction-calendar change control.

## Four approved synthetic underwriting assumptions

Treat these summaries as baseline memo evidence, not as independently inspected original records.

### A1

**Claim:** Segregated client funds and dependable transmission controls protect payroll execution and customer retention.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved underwriting memo's Treasury Controls paragraph summarizes designated accounts, daily reconciliations, and synthetic 2026 transmission records.

**Review criterion:** A client-fund account is used for corporate purposes or a qualifying transmission misses its deadline.

**Contract relationship:** direct; S1, C07, C08. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A2

**Claim:** Documented jurisdiction updates and tested releases limit calculation and filing errors as the client base expands.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved underwriting memo's Compliance Platform paragraph identifies the jurisdiction calendar, release gates, and historical exception process.

**Review criterion:** An overdue jurisdiction configuration, untested release, or material filing exception is identified.

**Contract relationship:** direct; S1, S2, S3. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A3

**Claim:** Recurring fee revenue and 28-day collections produce adequate cash coverage after taxes, investment, and debt service.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved underwriting memo's Cash Flow paragraph separates fee receivables from restricted client funds and presents the cash-coverage bridge.

**Review criterion:** Fee collections slow materially, required investment rises, or annual cash coverage approaches its minimum.

**Contract relationship:** direct; C02, C09. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A4

**Claim:** Moderate opening leverage and limited distributions protect refinancing capacity despite low scheduled amortization.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved underwriting memo's Debt Capacity paragraph reconciles opening net debt, Covenant EBITDA, amortization, and distribution conditions.

**Review criterion:** Leverage rises toward the maintenance limit or cash is used for nonoperating transactions.

**Contract relationship:** direct; C01, C04, C05. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

## Financial model conventions

The authored balance sheet records trade receivables of $8.438m at 28 collection days, other operating current assets of $14.275m, net fixed/software assets of $24.300m, and historical acquisition goodwill of $101.200m. These are book balances, not collateral recovery estimates. See the model's explicit prepaid/unbilled and deferred-billing simplifications.

- 2025 is comparative income and balance-sheet data; a 2025 cash-flow statement is not fabricated.
- 2026 historical restructuring expense is spread evenly across the four quarters; it is not forecast to recur in 2027.
- Predecessor debt has the same principal and fixed interest rate, with no 2026 amortization; refinancing at par adds no cash or fees.
- 2026 shareholder distributions or contributions reconcile the historical cash target and precede the new agreement.
- Payables and operating accruals (including accrued expenses and deferred customer billings) are held constant; receivables and other operating current assets absorb the stated working-capital change. Deferred customer billings are not forecast separately.
- Other operating current assets comprise prepaid expenses and unbilled contract assets; model movements are explicit, not new EBITDA addbacks.
- Historical goodwill represents earlier acquisitions and stays constant; fixed/software assets roll by capex less depreciation/amortization.
- Restricted cash is custodial client money with an equal segregated-funds liability, excluded from lender collateral, leverage netting and available operating cash.
- Forecast negative cash is an unfunded deficit, not an actual bank balance or assumed new loan; such a scenario is infeasible without corrective financing or spending changes.
- Cash tax is 25% of positive pretax income, with no loss benefit or deferred-tax complexity.
- Q1 realizations are synthetic, with no assumed submission timestamp; every actual delivery obligation must be tested separately.
- Annual interest uses ACT/365F; principal installments are effective December 31, so within-year principal is constant.
