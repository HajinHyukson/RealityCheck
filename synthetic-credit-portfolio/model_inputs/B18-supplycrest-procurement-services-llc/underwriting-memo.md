# SupplyCrest Procurement Services, LLC Underwriting memorandum

Memo B18-UW-2026-01. Synthetic approval and availability: 2026-12-31. Proposed for a private-credit analyst. Dollar amounts are millions. Future scenarios are forecasts known at origination, not later observations.

## Borrower and credit decision

SupplyCrest runs sourcing events, supplier onboarding, purchase-order administration, and spend analytics for multi-site businesses under recurring management fees and project charges. The model is asset light, but customer trust depends on delegated-authority limits, transparent supplier economics, and defensible savings calculations.

Opening net leverage is 3.18x on $26.1 million of Covenant EBITDA, and the 4.10x ceiling allows roughly a 22% EBITDA decline at constant net debt. The $5 million liquidity floor and $8 million forecast trigger protect against rapid client attrition or fee disputes while preserving an early-information threshold below opening cash. Asset-light operations and 35-day receivables support conversion, but SupplyCrest may not use customer purchasing funds or hidden supplier economics as liquidity. Transaction baskets and a $7 million cash floor preserve deleveraging capacity. The core weakness is agency risk: unauthorized commitments, supplier conflicts, or retained rebates can impair a major client relationship before current earnings or leverage reflects the damage.

### A1

**Claim:** Documented delegated authority permits efficient sourcing without unauthorized client commitments.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved December 31, 2026 memo's Procurement Controls paragraph summarizes synthetic authority matrices, exception history, and client approvals.

**Review criterion:** A commitment above authority, repeated post-execution approval, or a disputed supplier award.

**Contract relationship:** reporting; S1, S2, C08. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A2

**Claim:** Supplier economics are transparent and customer purchasing funds remain outside SupplyCrest's assets.

**Baseline evidence:** The memo's Agency Economics paragraph describes fee terms, rebate treatment, invoice disclosures, and client payment flows.

**Review criterion:** An undisclosed rebate, supplier payment to an affiliate, or customer-fund receipt.

**Contract relationship:** direct; S1, S3, C07. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A3

**Claim:** Supplier selection controls limit conflicts that could damage recurring client relationships.

**Baseline evidence:** The memo's Supplier Governance paragraph identifies conflict certifications, bid documentation, and affiliate approvals.

**Review criterion:** An undisclosed affiliation, missing bid record, or client challenge to award integrity.

**Contract relationship:** direct; S2, S3. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A4

**Claim:** Recurring management fees and retained cash flow support debt service without using client purchase flows.

**Baseline evidence:** The memo's Repayment paragraph reconciles fee collections and operating uses to amortization and the maturity balloon.

**Review criterion:** Client nonrenewal, disputed fees, or eligible cash reaching the forecast trigger.

**Contract relationship:** direct; C01, C09, C04. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

## Financial model conventions

The authored balance sheet records trade receivables of $12.658m at 35 collection days, other operating current assets of $16.502m, net fixed/software assets of $27.800m, and historical acquisition goodwill of $106.950m. These are book balances, not collateral recovery estimates. See the model's explicit prepaid/unbilled and deferred-billing simplifications.

- 2025 is comparative income and balance-sheet data; a 2025 cash-flow statement is not fabricated.
- 2026 historical restructuring expense is spread evenly across the four quarters; it is not forecast to recur in 2027.
- Predecessor debt has the same principal and fixed interest rate, with no 2026 amortization; refinancing at par adds no cash or fees.
- 2026 shareholder distributions or contributions reconcile the historical cash target and precede the new agreement.
- Payables and operating accruals (including accrued expenses and deferred customer billings) are held constant; receivables and other operating current assets absorb the stated working-capital change. Deferred customer billings are not forecast separately.
- Other operating current assets comprise prepaid expenses and unbilled contract assets; model movements are explicit, not new EBITDA addbacks.
- Historical goodwill represents earlier acquisitions and stays constant; fixed/software assets roll by capex less depreciation/amortization.
- Restricted cash is borrower-owned cash reserved by contract for operating performance obligations, not client money; it has no matching client-funds liability and no competing lien. It is excluded from leverage netting and available cash.
- Forecast negative cash is an unfunded deficit, not an actual bank balance or assumed new loan; such a scenario is infeasible without corrective financing or spending changes.
- Cash tax is 25% of positive pretax income, with no loss benefit or deferred-tax complexity.
- Q1 realizations are synthetic, with no assumed submission timestamp; every actual delivery obligation must be tested separately.
- Annual interest uses ACT/365F; principal installments are effective December 31, so within-year principal is constant.
