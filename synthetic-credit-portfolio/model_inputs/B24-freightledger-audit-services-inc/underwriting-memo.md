# FreightLedger Audit Services, Inc. Underwriting memorandum

Memo B24-UW-2026-01. Synthetic approval and availability: 2026-12-31. Proposed for a private-credit analyst. Dollar amounts are millions. Future scenarios are forecasts known at origination, not later observations.

## Borrower and credit decision

FreightLedger validates carrier invoices, identifies billing exceptions, and administers approved freight payments for large shippers under transaction and subscription fees. Segregation of client payment funds and backlog growth in unresolved invoice exceptions are its distinctive operating risks.

Opening net debt is $85 million after the contractual cash-netting cap, producing 3.01x leverage on $28.2 million of Covenant EBITDA. The 4.00x ceiling permits about a 25% EBITDA decline. A $7 million liquidity floor and $10 million forecast trigger address the timing exposure created by payroll and operating costs during slower customer collections; $8 million of client-controlled funds is restricted and never treated as eligible cash. Transaction permissions require $9 million of post-closing Eligible Cash. The key vulnerability is operational: a growing invoice-exception backlog can delay approvals and fee recognition, while any commingling of payment funds could impair customer trust and create restitution demands. The adjustment is a documented cash cost from consolidating duplicate processing teams.

## Four approved synthetic underwriting assumptions

Baseline evidence descriptions below are authored source summaries within this simulated approved memo. Original bank statements, system exports and historical customer agreements are not separately reproduced unless explicitly supplied as evidence files. Treat these summaries as baseline memo evidence, not as independently inspected original records.

### A1

**Claim:** Client payment funds remain segregated from operating cash and are released only through controlled payment batches.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved memorandum's funds-flow paragraph cites titled bank accounts, sample reconciliations, and dual-approval records.

**Review criterion:** Any operating use of client funds or a material payment batch lacking reconciliation and independent approval.

**Contract relationship:** direct; S1, S3. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A2

**Claim:** Invoice exceptions are resolved promptly enough to preserve customer confidence and transaction-fee conversion.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved memorandum's operations paragraph cites the December 2026 exception-aging export and resolution workflow.

**Review criterion:** Two consecutive quarterly increases in aged exceptions or a material customer dispute over payment accuracy.

**Contract relationship:** reporting; S2, S3. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A3

**Claim:** Operating liquidity is adequate without relying on restricted client payment balances.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved memorandum's liquidity paragraph separately reconciles Eligible Cash and client-controlled bank balances.

**Review criterion:** Eligible Cash reaches the forecast trigger or any restricted balance appears in cash netting.

**Contract relationship:** direct; C01, C09, S1. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

### A4

**Claim:** Leverage headroom can absorb ordinary customer-volume and processing-cost variability.

**Baseline evidence:** The approved memorandum's capitalization paragraph cites opening net debt, Covenant EBITDA, and constant-debt downside capacity.

**Review criterion:** Quarterly leverage rises above the opening level or the exception backlog indicates sustained margin pressure.

**Contract relationship:** analyst_judgment; C01, S2. These links describe possible protection and do not add a covenant.

## Financial model conventions

The authored balance sheet records trade receivables of $18.274m at 46 collection days, other operating current assets of $19.546m, net fixed/software assets of $35.700m, and historical acquisition goodwill of $109.250m. These are book balances, not collateral recovery estimates. See the model's explicit prepaid/unbilled and deferred-billing simplifications.

- 2025 is comparative income and balance-sheet data; a 2025 cash-flow statement is not fabricated.
- 2026 historical restructuring expense is spread evenly across the four quarters; it is not forecast to recur in 2027.
- Predecessor debt has the same principal and fixed interest rate, with no 2026 amortization; refinancing at par adds no cash or fees.
- 2026 shareholder distributions or contributions reconcile the historical cash target and precede the new agreement.
- Payables and operating accruals (including accrued expenses and deferred customer billings) are held constant; receivables and other operating current assets absorb the stated working-capital change. Deferred customer billings are not forecast separately.
- Other operating current assets comprise prepaid expenses and unbilled contract assets; model movements are explicit, not new EBITDA addbacks.
- Historical goodwill represents earlier acquisitions and stays constant; fixed/software assets roll by capex less depreciation/amortization.
- Restricted cash is custodial client money with an equal segregated-funds liability, excluded from lender collateral, leverage netting and available operating cash.
- Forecast negative cash is an unfunded deficit, not an actual bank balance or assumed new loan; such a scenario is infeasible without corrective financing or spending changes.
- Cash tax is 25% of positive pretax income, with no loss benefit or deferred-tax complexity.
- Q1 realizations are synthetic, with no assumed submission timestamp; every actual delivery obligation must be tested separately.
- Annual interest uses ACT/365F; principal installments are effective December 31, so within-year principal is constant.
