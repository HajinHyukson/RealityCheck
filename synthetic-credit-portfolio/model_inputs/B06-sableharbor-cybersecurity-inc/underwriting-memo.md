# SableHarbor Cybersecurity, Inc. Underwriting memorandum

Memo B06-UW-2026-01. Synthetic approval and availability: 2026-12-31. Proposed for a private-credit analyst. Dollar amounts are millions. Future scenarios are forecasts known at origination, not later observations.

## Borrower and credit decision

SableHarbor sells privileged-access management software and managed identity monitoring to financial and industrial customers under multi-year subscriptions. Its high margins depend on secure administrative access, controlled cryptographic keys, and credible incident handling across its hosted service.

The 4.80x maintenance limit provides about 22% EBITDA decline capacity from 3.74x opening net leverage. No optional ratio is added because strong recurring billings and margins provide cash visibility, but one-percent amortization creates substantial maturity refinancing dependence. The $10 million transaction floor and tighter distribution leverage test preserve incident-response and research capacity. The central vulnerability is trust: misuse of privileged production credentials or encryption-key custody can prompt rapid customer action before reported revenue or leverage responds. Specialized covenants impose administrative-access controls, defined security-incident reporting, and a prior-consent prohibition on delegating cryptographic root authority. Acquisition capacity permits targeted product expansion while requiring pro forma leverage below the maintenance ceiling.

## Financial model conventions

The authored balance sheet records trade receivables of $25.849m at 51 collection days, other operating current assets of $22.785m, net fixed/software assets of $56.400m, and historical acquisition goodwill of $247.250m. These are book balances, not collateral recovery estimates. See the model's explicit prepaid/unbilled and deferred-billing simplifications.

- 2025 is comparative income and balance-sheet data; a 2025 cash-flow statement is not fabricated.
- 2026 historical restructuring expense is spread evenly across the four quarters; it is not forecast to recur in 2027.
- Predecessor debt has the same principal and fixed interest rate, with no 2026 amortization; refinancing at par adds no cash or fees.
- 2026 shareholder distributions or contributions reconcile the historical cash target and precede the new agreement.
- Payables and operating accruals (including accrued expenses and deferred customer billings) are held constant; receivables and other operating current assets absorb the stated working-capital change. Deferred customer billings are not forecast separately.
- Other operating current assets comprise prepaid expenses and unbilled contract assets; model movements are explicit, not new EBITDA addbacks.
- Historical goodwill represents earlier acquisitions and stays constant; fixed/software assets roll by capex less depreciation/amortization.
- Restricted cash is borrower-owned cash reserved by contract for operating performance obligations, not client money; it has no matching client-funds liability and no competing lien. It is excluded from leverage netting and available cash.
- Forecast negative cash is an unfunded deficit, not an actual bank balance or assumed new loan; such a scenario is infeasible without corrective financing or spending changes.
- Cash tax is 25% of positive pretax income, with no loss benefit or deferred-tax complexity.
- Q1 realizations are synthetic, with no assumed submission timestamp; every actual delivery obligation must be tested separately.
- Annual interest uses ACT/365F; principal installments are effective December 31, so within-year principal is constant.
