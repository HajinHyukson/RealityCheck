# Ironwood Field Inspection, LLC Underwriting memorandum

Memo B20-UW-2026-01. Synthetic approval and availability: 2026-12-31. Proposed for a private-credit analyst. Dollar amounts are millions. Future scenarios are forecasts known at origination, not later observations.

## Borrower and credit decision

Ironwood performs visual condition surveys, construction-progress observations, and asset inventory inspections for utilities and commercial property owners, without providing engineering design or certification. Revenue is project and route based, while report credibility depends on qualified inspectors, calibrated tools, and traceable field evidence.

Ironwood opens at 2.93x net leverage on $19.8 million of Covenant EBITDA, and the 3.80x limit affords about a 23% EBITDA decline at constant net debt. The $4.8 million annual capital-investment cap exceeds the $3.8 million base requirement for vehicles, devices, and field-data software while limiting discretionary spending. A $6.5 million transaction cash floor reflects technician payroll and a 55-day receivable cycle. Moderate leverage is important because project delays can strand fleet and labor. Underwriting relies on repeat routes, traceable evidence, inspector qualifications, and clear limits on the firm's non-engineering scope. The key vulnerability is unreliable field evidence or expired qualifications that force reinspections and undermine customer acceptance before financial covenants show deterioration.

**Claim:** Ironwood remains within observation services and does not assume unauthorized engineering certification risk.

**Review criterion:** Work outside authorized observation scope, an unauthorized engineering certification, or repeated scope disputes.

### A4

**Claim:** Route density and retained operating cash support investment, amortization, and eventual refinancing.

## Financial model conventions

The authored balance sheet records trade receivables of $15.822m at 55 collection days, other operating current assets of $13.528m, net fixed/software assets of $31.800m, and historical acquisition goodwill of $77.050m. These are book balances, not collateral recovery estimates. See the model's explicit prepaid/unbilled and deferred-billing simplifications.

- 2025 is comparative income and balance-sheet data; a 2025 cash-flow statement is not fabricated.
- 2026 historical restructuring expense is spread evenly across the four quarters; it is not forecast to recur in 2027.
- Predecessor debt has the same principal and fixed interest rate, with no 2026 amortization; refinancing at par adds no cash or fees.
- 2026 shareholder distributions or contributions reconcile the historical cash target and precede the new agreement.
- Payables and operating accruals (including accrued expenses and deferred customer billings) are held constant; receivables and other operating current assets absorb the stated working-capital change. Deferred customer billings are not forecast separately.
- Other operating current assets comprise prepaid expenses and unbilled contract assets; model movements are explicit, not new EBITDA addbacks.
- Historical goodwill represents earlier acquisitions and stays constant; fixed/software assets roll by capex less depreciation/amortization.
- Restricted cash is borrower-owned cash reserved by contract for operating performance obligations, not client money; it has no matching client-funds liability and no competing lien. It is excluded from leverage netting and available cash.
- Forecast negative cash is an unfunded deficit, not an actual bank balance or assumed new loan; such a scenario is infeasible without corrective financing or spending changes.
- Cash tax is 25% of positive pretax income, with no loss benefit or deferred-tax complexity.
- Q1 realizations are synthetic, with no assumed submission timestamp; every actual delivery obligation must be tested separately.
- Annual interest uses ACT/365F; principal installments are effective December 31, so within-year principal is constant.
